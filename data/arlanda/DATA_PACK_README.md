# Stockholm Arlanda SAF case data

**Case date** Tuesday 17 November 2026, Europe/Stockholm  
**Cohort** 12 SAS-group departures from ARN on five routes, with 12 matched public ADS-B tracks  
**Modelled intervention** A320-251N / two CFM LEAP-1A26 engines and a 35%-by-mass HEFA-SPK/Jet A-1 delivery blend

Use this pack with the Stockholm Arlanda app brief. Load `flight_manifest.csv` first and join every flight-specific table on `study_id`. The historical cohort defines the comparison. Fuel allocation, alternate trajectories and savings are counterfactual model outputs.

## How the cohort was selected

The team brief supplied a date, a target aircraft and five destinations. I selected 12 publicly documented services with a matching A320-251N airframe and an open track. The mix is three Copenhagen, three Oslo, three Heathrow, two Paris and one Berlin. The frame type is supported by the dated tracker or registration record. The LEAP-1A26 is the study's modelled engine. The exact installed subvariant was unverified tail by tail. Operating carrier is populated where the dated evidence identifies it. [EASA A320 type certificate data sheet](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/16507/en) · [17 November ARN schedule](https://www.flightsfrom.com/ARN/departures?departureDate=2026-11-17)

Multiple sources agree on SK1415's SE-ROX registration and an archived Copenhagen track. I selected it after SK1417's public history gave conflicting tail information. SK531's Plane Finder flight page and PlaneMapper report different times. The open track places the first observed airborne point at 10:43 UTC. The manifest preserves the source event and the discrepancy. Use the raw position timestamps for retrospective sampling. One isolated 59,200 ft point was removed from SK1407, logged in `tracks/rejected_points.csv`, and retained in the audit trail.

## Working with the weather

- The **forecast initialisations** are 16 November 18:00 UTC and 17 November 00:00, 06:00 and 12:00 UTC. Pick information available at each decision. The provider's index timestamp is a public availability proxy. A separate column tests that proxy against an illustrative cutoff 30 minutes before scheduled departure. For an early fleet-wide fuel deadline, the 16 November 18:00 cycle offers a practical starting point, subject to the deadline in the case. [NOAA 17 November GFS archive](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/pub/data/nccf/com/gfs/prod/gfs.20261117/00/atmos/) · [NOAA GFS documentation](https://www.nco.ncep.noaa.gov/pmb/products/gfs/nomads/)
- The GRIB2 subsets cover 46–64°N and 8°W–27°E on a 0.25° grid at 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 450 and 500 hPa. Pressure-level fields include temperature, specific humidity, zonal and meridional winds, vertical velocity, geopotential height, cloud liquid and ice mixing ratios. Forecast files also contain top-of-atmosphere outgoing shortwave and longwave fluxes. Use the weather index to keep forecast cycles distinct. The 0-hour analysis files are suitable for retrospective model comparison. [GFS in pycontrails](https://py.contrails.org/notebooks/GFS.html)
- The derived CSV uses one observed position per minute, a standard-atmosphere pressure approximation, bilinear horizontal interpolation, log-pressure vertical interpolation and linear interpolation in forecast-valid time. Relative humidity over ice is vapour pressure divided by ice saturation vapour pressure. The higher and lower columns support a local weather sensitivity screen. A candidate flight plan also needs climb, descent, fuel and airspace feasibility. Thin ice-supersaturated layers can be smoothed by this grid. [Ice saturation pressure method](https://doi.org/10.1256/qj.04.94)
- Forecast updates show how the deterministic prediction changed during the day. For calibrated weather scenarios, use an ensemble or historical forecast-error replay with a dated validation sample. The four model analyses are a retrospective reference. [NOAA GEFS ensemble archive](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-gefs/)

## Remaining model and operator inputs

1. **Flight energy and fuel.** Obtain payload, dispatch route, actual block/trip fuel, reserves and remaining fuel from SAS where available. Otherwise use a documented aircraft-performance model on the reference and alternate profiles, with assumed payload, winds and reserve policy. The EASA engine table is a ground certification reference. The published 2026 taxi-plume study on the A320-251N provides a measured particle-emissions response under its test conditions. [Schmitt et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12969261/) · [OpenAP](https://github.com/junzis/openap)
2. **Airport stock and delivery.** Ask for opening stock, replenishments, finished fuel volume, stand/truck slots, booking deadlines, service time, physical uplift and the certified batch intensity or charges. The 2023 segregated-delivery trial provides evidence of a dedicated truck, approximately 8,000 L in that trial, and a roughly 35%-by-mass blend. At the example densities a full 8,000 L load would contain about 2.20 tonnes of neat SAF. Use that as a sensitivity scale labelled as an assumption. [Quante et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jatrs.2024.100049)
3. **Climate estimates.** For each flight/route/fuel/weather combination, calculate net signed contrail energy forcing in J using an appropriate CoCiP configuration and a stated engine–fuel response sensitivity, including zero response. Use the 2025–36 EU policy series for project scale context. The two main outputs of the optimisation pipeline are route energy in MJ and net contrail energy forcing in J. [CoCiP forecast method](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/253/2025/) · [Cascade ReFuelEU method](https://docs.cascade.boeing.com/docs/scenarios/scenarios/refuelEU.html) · [Official ReFuelEU Cascade scenario](https://app.cascade.boeing.com/forecast?view=0bx1i&scenarioID=G8)

The reference scenario uses an assumed zero-SAF conventional delivery. The 35% mass fraction applies to the special delivery, so 1,000 kg delivers 350 kg of neat SAF. It corresponds to approximately 36.18% by volume at the two assumed densities. Use the fuel mass burned for life-cycle emissions, the physical fuel uplift for inventory, and the mixed onboard fraction for the engine-response model.
